### 🧠 Product Segmentation Documentation

#### 1. Objective

The goal of this segmentation is to group products into **six performance tiers (1–6)** based on their commercial, customer, and behavioral performance. Each tier represents a cluster of products with similar performance characteristics derived through **K-Means clustering**.

---

#### 2. Features Used and Their Purpose

The following features were selected based on their analytical importance and contribution to understanding product behavior:

##### **Financial Performance**

* **TotalRevenue** – Measures total monetary value generated by each product.
* **TotalProfit** – Reflects profitability and cost efficiency.
* **ProfitMargin** – Ratio of profit to revenue; shows profitability quality.
* **TotalCost** – Tracks the overall expense associated with product sales.

##### **Pricing Behavior**

* **UnitPrice_mean_x** – Average selling price, indicating market positioning.
* **PriceVolatility** – Variability in pricing; high volatility may indicate unstable demand or inconsistent strategy.

##### **Demand & Sales Dynamics**

* **SalesQty_sum** – Total units sold; indicator of demand.
* **SalesFrequency** – Average rate of product sales; frequency of transactions.
* **AvgOrderQuantity** – Typical quantity ordered per transaction.
* **ProductLifetimeDays** – Number of days since first recorded sale; reflects maturity and longevity.

##### **Customer Engagement**

* **CustomerBase** – Number of unique customers; breadth of adoption.
* **CustomerPenetration** – Percentage of customer reach; relative engagement.
* **RevenuePerCustomer** – Indicates revenue efficiency per customer.
* **OrdersPerCustomer** – Average number of orders per customer; shows loyalty and repeat behavior.

##### **Quoting & Conversion Behavior**

* **QuoteConversionRate** – Ratio of quotes converted to orders.
* **QuoteWinRate** – Measures sales competitiveness.
* **AvgQuoteNegotiations** – Average quote revisions before closing a deal; higher numbers indicate longer sales cycles or friction.

Each category contributes a different perspective:

* Financial → Value creation
* Pricing → Market positioning
* Demand → Sales traction
* Customer → Market depth
* Quoting → Sales efficiency

---

#### 3. Mathematical Basis of K-Means Segmentation

**K-Means Clustering** is an **unsupervised machine learning algorithm** that groups data points (products) into *k* clusters based on feature similarity.

##### Step-by-Step:

1. **Input**: Each product is represented as an *n-dimensional vector* of standardized features.
   [ x = [x_1, x_2, x_3, ..., x_n] ]

2. **Initialization**: K-Means randomly chooses *k* cluster centroids (in this case, k = 6).

3. **Assignment**: Each product is assigned to the nearest centroid using **Euclidean distance**:
   [ D(x, μ_k) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - μ_{k,i})^2} ]

4. **Update**: The centroid of each cluster is recalculated as the mean of all points in that cluster.
   [ μ_k = \frac{1}{N_k} \sum_{x_i \in C_k} x_i ]

5. **Iteration**: Steps 3 and 4 repeat until centroids stabilize (minimal change in cluster assignments).

The final output assigns each product to a **Tier (1–6)**, where each tier represents a cluster of products with similar behavioral and financial characteristics.

---

#### 4. Tier Interpretation Logic

After clustering, each cluster (Tier) is analyzed by its centroid values to understand what defines it.

We compute three key composite metrics from normalized centroid data:

* **PerformanceScore** = 0.4 × TotalRevenue + 0.4 × TotalProfit + 0.2 × ProfitMargin
  → Captures overall financial strength.

* **DemandScore** = 0.5 × SalesQty_sum + 0.3 × SalesFrequency + 0.2 × CustomerBase
  → Measures product popularity and movement velocity.

* **StabilityScore** = (1 - PriceVolatility) × 0.7 + (1 - AvgQuoteNegotiations) × 0.3
  → Indicates pricing and sales process stability.

Based on these scores, each cluster is categorized as one of the following:

| Tier Type                      | Characteristics                                                             |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **High Performer / Flagship**  | High PerformanceScore, High DemandScore; strong profitability and adoption. |
| **Premium Niche Product**      | High PerformanceScore, Low DemandScore; specialized, high-value items.      |
| **High Volume / Low Margin**   | Low PerformanceScore, High DemandScore; popular but low-margin products.    |
| **Low Performer / At Risk**    | Low PerformanceScore and DemandScore; poor revenue and traction.            |
| **Unstable / Price Sensitive** | Low StabilityScore; volatile pricing or inconsistent sales process.         |
| **Mid Performer / Stable**     | Balanced metrics; consistent but not exceptional performers.                |

* **product_segmentation_labeled.csv** – Final dataset with `ProductID`, `Tier`, and `TierLabel`.
* **Tier Summary Table** – Average feature values and composite scores per tier.
* **Visualization** – Cluster size and centroid heatmap for interpretability.

#### 6. Business Insights

* Tiers allow for **targeted product strategy** (pricing optimization, promotion, or phase-out decisions).
* Helps **identify top revenue drivers** and **underperforming SKUs**.
* Enables **data-driven lifecycle management**: whether to scale, adjust pricing, or discontinue products.

This segmentation provides a quantitative foundation for strategic decision-making by combining economic, behavioral, and customer-based signals. Each tier is grounded in measurable mathematical clustering while offering interpretable business meaning.
